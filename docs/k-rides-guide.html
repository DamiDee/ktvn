Before you start

Accounts, signing in, and who can do what

K-Rides works the same on a phone and a laptop — there is nothing to install. New members create their own account from Sign up; every other role is created for you by the oversight team, so if you are an admin, a coordinator or a driver, wait to be added rather than signing up.

Sign in at /login with your email or username and your password. K-Rides then takes you straight to the part of the app your role owns, so you never have to hunt for your own home screen. The Sign out button lives at the bottom of the sidebar on a computer, and inside the account sheet on a phone.

Part one

For members

Two things matter to you: getting a seat, and being able to show it at the door. Everything else in your view exists to support those two.

Booking a seat

  1. Choose your direction

    Open Find a bus and pick To service or Going home. The list changes to match.

  2. Pick your stop

    Only stops that actually have a bus are offered, so whatever you can tap has a seat behind it. Journeys are then grouped by day, soonest first.

  3. Confirm the seat

    Tap a journey and confirm. K-Rides re-checks the departure and the remaining capacity with the server at that moment, picks a bus for you, and assigns your seat number.

  4. Keep your pass

    You are taken straight to your boarding pass — a QR code with your seat, bus and departure. Find it again any time under Boarding passes.

At the door. Show the QR code on your pass, or hand over the NFC tag a coordinator gave you. Either one boards you in a second. If your phone is flat, a coordinator can find you by name on the bus manifest instead.

If there is no bus on the route you need

Open Ask for a route, choose the route and the day you need it — any day of the week — and send it. Your interest joins everyone else's on that route, and oversight sees the demand as a number and decides how many buses to put on. Routes are sorted by how many people have already asked, so you can see where the pressure is. If a bus is already running on that route and day, the page tells you so and sends you to book instead.

Your own history

My journeys collects everywhere you have travelled with the community — past trips, seats and routes. Profile holds your details and your sign-out. And if your plans change, release the seat from your pass: it frees it for someone else at once, and K-Rides warns you first, because your pass stops working the moment you do.

Part two

For Admin and Root

You build the service in layers, and the order matters: points are real places, routes join two points and are reused forever, trips are a single departure on a route, and a bus is what carries it. Get the first two right once and the weekly work becomes scheduling alone.

Setting up, once

  1. Add your boarding points

    Under Points, drop a pin on the map and describe the spot the way a member standing there would need it — the landmark to look for and which side of the road to wait on. Picking a place on the map fills in a first draft for you to edit.

  2. Create each route once

    Under Routes, name it, choose the boarding point and the destination, and add any stops along the way in the order the bus will call at them. The distance in kilometres is measured along that exact path, so reordering stops changes it. Write the boarding instructions members will read before they travel — a starting draft appears as soon as you choose a boarding point.

  3. Register the fleet

    Under Buses, add each vehicle with its plate and seat count. A bus stays in the fleet permanently; you assign it to a different trip whenever you need it.

The weekly rhythm

  1. Read the demand

    Ride Requests shows how many members have asked for each route on each day. That number is what tells you how many buses a route needs.

  2. Schedule the departures

    Under Trips, add a departure on an existing route for a date and time — any day of the week, as many as you like, without ever recreating the route.

  3. Put buses on them

    Assign a bus to a trip from Buses. From a ride request, Assign buses takes you straight there with the right trip already selected; if no trip exists yet, it offers to schedule one first. Seats open for booking the moment a bus is assigned.

  4. Watch the day run

    Overview counts your buses, upcoming trips and meeting points and links to the four things you do most. Start, cancel or delete an individual trip from Trips — a change there affects only that departure, never the reusable route.

People

System Users is where every account lives. Create staff accounts directly — Member, Driver, Route Coordinator, Admin or Root — change someone's role, deactivate an account that should stop working, reactivate it later, or delete it. Your own row is locked so you cannot lock yourself out. Only a Root account may grant or withdraw Admin and Root access; an Admin sees the other roles only.

The audit trail

Activity is the server's own record of who changed what and when, searchable and filterable. It is the first place to look when something on the service is not as you left it.

Nothing destructive happens quietly. Deleting, cancelling, clearing, deactivating and role changes all stop for a confirmation that names the thing by name and spells out the consequence — including when it cannot be undone, and when the server may refuse (a trip with bookings on it, for instance). Read it, then confirm.

Part three

For Route Coordinators

Your job happens at the door of a bus, so your view is deliberately narrow: two sections, and both of them are about getting the right people aboard. You sign in and land on boarding already.

Boarding a bus

  1. Find your bus

    Buses lists the fleet with its plate, seats and state. Open bookings on the bus you are working takes you to its manifest.

  2. Choose the bus, then start the scanner

    On Bookings & boarding, select the boarding bus and start the scanner once. Camera or NFC permission is asked for once, at the start.

  3. Scan each pass

    A verified pass for this bus boards the passenger automatically — no extra tap per person. The result of every scan appears immediately above the manifest, and a refused pass says why. QR works everywhere; NFC tags work on supported Android Chrome devices.

  4. Or work the manifest by hand

    Switch to Search manifest and the scanner stops. Search by name, booking reference or seat, and board a passenger with the Board button on their row. This is your path when a phone is flat or a camera will not cooperate.

The manifest shows every booking on the bus with its seat, passenger, reference and status, and it sorts and searches — so a full bus is still quick to work through. As a last resort you can expand Board by typing a reference below the manifest; check the passenger against the list first, because a signed QR or NFC pass is the real proof.

If the manifest will not load. A coordinator's access to bookings is granted on the server, not in this app. If you see a permissions message, the backend team needs to enable boarding access for Route Coordinators — no setting in K-Rides can override it.
